## Claim
From the article's opening: the most-quoted recent coaching study comes from
Bent Rønnestad's lab in Lillehammer. Published in the *Scandinavian Journal
of Medicine & Science in Sports*, its finding is that short, hard intervals
improve VO2max, peak power and FTP more than an equivalent block of long
endurance riding.

Research check (abstracts, not the full article):
- 2015, trained cyclists, 10 weeks: 30 s on / 15 s off vs effort-matched
  5-minute efforts. VO2max rose 8.7% vs 2.6%. Groups of 9 and 7.
- 2020, elite cyclists, 3 weeks: short intervals raised peak aerobic power
  3.7% vs -0.3% for long intervals, with no VO2max difference. A 20-minute
  test rose 4.7% vs -1.4%.

Still not captured: the 2-hour vs 5-hour long-ride comparison in the
headline, and the article's own recommendations.

## How we apply it
- Put the main power-building work into short, hard intervals (30 s–5 min
  efforts) in the Mon–Wed indoor sessions.
- Treat the long weekend ride as endurance and durability work, not the main
  source of power gains.

## Limits
- Both studies are small, short (3–10 weeks), and used trained riders. The
  effects are shown on power measures, not reliably on VO2max.
- Event demands still apply. A 145 km, 1,426 m ride with a sub-6-hour target
  needs some long rides close to event duration in the final build, for
  durability, pacing and fuelling practice.
